# Voice and Tone

## Voice (consistent across all content)

Chainguard's voice has four defining traits:

- **Confident.** Clarity and authority, grounded in deep expertise. Never boastful, but always sure of the value provided.
- **Quirky.** Wit, humor, and not taking ourselves too seriously. Keeps content fresh and human.
- **Empowering.** Focused on lifting up engineering and security teams — giving them the knowledge and resources to do meaningful work.
- **Built by engineers, for engineers.** Practical and straightforward. A trusted peer who's been there.

When reviewing for voice, ask:
- Does it sound like a confident expert, or like it's trying too hard to sell?
- Is there any personality — or does it read like generic B2B SaaS copy?
- Does it empower the reader, or talk down to them?
- Would a senior engineer respect the author, or roll their eyes?

## Tone (moves on a spectrum based on channel)

Tone shifts based on who the content is for, why, and where it lives. Same voice, different energy.

**Lighter / more playful — turn the quirk up:**
- Social posts
- Campaign headlines and taglines
- Top-of-funnel content like "This Shit Is Hard" blog posts
- Memorable brand experiences (event booths, etc.)

**More direct / credible — turn the quirk down:**
- Research and data-driven guides
- Product documentation and updates
- Sales enablement materials
- Executive thought leadership
- Direct email communication

The rule of thumb: **engagement and memorability → playful. Credibility and clarity → direct.**

Do not confuse "direct" with "boring" or "playful" with "sloppy." Even the most serious docs can have a moment of personality; even the snarkiest social post should be clear.

## Guiding principles

### Remember your audience

- Understand them deeply and speak directly to their challenges and goals.
- Frame the message around the value they'll gain.

### Be a trusted resource

- Back claims and insights with data.
- Cite customer examples and SME/thought-leader quotes where possible.
- Don't focus on the product — focus on the problem.
- Provide actionable insights. Position Chainguard as the logical next step, not the hero of every sentence.

### Tailor to the channel

- Social and email: bullets, short paragraphs.
- Blog: narrative flow.
- Longer form: clear sections and callouts.

### Edit AI-generated content for humanity

- Refine tone to match Chainguard's voice.
- Fact-check — product messaging changes.
- Eliminate bland fluff. "In today's modern world," "In the ever-evolving landscape of," "leverage," "unlock," "empower your journey" — cut them.

## Inclusivity

- **Gender-neutral.** "They" not "he/she." "Teams" not "guys." "Parental leave" not "maternity leave."
- **No old stereotypes.** Coding isn't a male domain. No "basement-dweller" imagery.
- **Diverse examples.** When using names, choose a mix.
- **Global readability.** Avoid idioms that won't translate. No "i.e." or "e.g." — write it out.
- **Startup-to-enterprise balance.** Keep examples flexible; a startup and a Fortune 500 reader should both see themselves. Lead with universal value (reliability, trust, innovation).
