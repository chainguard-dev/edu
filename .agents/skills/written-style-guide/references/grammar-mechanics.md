# Grammar and Mechanics

Default reference: **The Chicago Manual of Style**. Default dictionary: **Merriam-Webster**. Default spelling: **US English**.

## Abbreviations and acronyms

- **No periods** between letters: CVE, not C.V.E.
- **Job title acronyms in all caps:** CEO, CTO, VP.
- **First usage:** write the full name with the acronym in parentheses, e.g., "Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVEs)." Then use the acronym from then on.
- **Exception:** if the term is used only once, don't include the acronym.
- **Don't use "i.e." or "e.g."** — write it out (global readability).

## Active voice

- Prefer active voice. The subject performs the action, not receives it.
- Warning signs of passive voice: "by [someone]," "is/was ___ed."
- Active aligns with the directness in Chainguard's brand voice.

## Spelling

- US English. "Color" not "colour." "Labor" not "labour." "Organization" not "organisation."
- Default dictionary: Merriam-Webster.

## Ampersand (&)

- **Don't use.** Spell out "and."
- **Exception:** proper nouns like "Dolce & Gabbana," "H&M."

## Capitalization

- **Chainguard** is always capitalized.
- **Named Chainguard products/features/teams** are capitalized; the generic topic is lowercase.
  - "I use Chainguard Containers." / "I use containers."
  - "Chainguard offers Custom Assembly." / "…allows for the custom assembly of container images."
- **Job titles** only capitalize when referring to a specific person.
  - "Chainguard Principal Software Engineer Jason Hall wrote the blog."
  - "The principal software engineer wrote the blog."
- **"This Shit Is Hard"** — always capitalized when referring to the blog series.

### Headings and UI element capitalization

**For web content:**
- H1 (headlines): sentence case
- H2 (sub-headlines): sentence case
- H3 (copy headings, callouts): sentence case
- Navigation and menu items: title case
- Buttons / CTAs: sentence case

**For reports and PDF assets:**
- H1: title case
- H2: title case
- H3: sentence case
- Buttons / CTAs: sentence case

**Sentence case rule:** Capitalize the first letter of the headline, the first letter after a colon, and proper nouns. Leave everything else lowercase.

## Punctuation

### Colons

- Use a colon to start a list.
- Use a colon to join two related phrases. If a complete sentence follows, capitalize the first word after the colon.

### Commas (Oxford comma)

- **Always use the Oxford comma**: "A, B, and C" (not "A, B and C").

### Contractions

- **Encouraged.** He's, can't, would've. They're part of the conversational, human voice.

### Ellipses (…)

- Three dots. Space after the ellipsis, no spaces before or between the dots.
- If the ellipsis follows a grammatically complete sentence, put it after the ending punctuation.
- Use for suspense sparingly — it loses effect quickly.

### Emojis

- Sparingly, for the right audience.
- **Acceptable channels:** social media, emails to non-executive audiences.
- Useful for people-driven content (LinkedIn posts about the team).
- Muted emojis can draw attention to key points.

### Hyphens and dashes

Three distinct marks:

- **Hyphen ( - )** — for compound words only. Example: "read-only."
- **En dash ( – )** — for ranges. Example: "2020–2025." On Mac: Option + Minus.
- **Em dash ( — )** — for breaking up text. **Spaces on either side.** Example: "The ancient oak — a true marvel — stood majestically." On Mac: Shift + Option + Minus.

Note: Chainguard uses spaces around em dashes, which differs from Chicago's default of no spaces.

### Ending punctuation

- **Avoid exclamation points.**
- One space after ending punctuation, not two.
- Headlines and subheadlines: no ending punctuation unless it's a question or a serial statement.
- Navigation, menu items, CTAs, buttons: no ending punctuation.

### Quotation marks and punctuation

- Periods, question marks, and exclamation marks **inside** the closing quotation mark: "What do you want to eat?"
- **Exception:** If you ask a question that ends with a quote, the question mark goes outside: What do you think of Dan's quote, "We win or we learn."?

### Spacing

- One space after punctuation. Never two.

## Numbers and percentages

- Spell out numbers **zero through nine** (one, two, three).
- Use numerals for **10 onward** (11, 12, 13).
- **Avoid starting a sentence with a number.** If unavoidable, spell it out: "Three people said…"
- OK to start a **subhead** with a number for emphasis. Don't capitalize the first word after: "95% of people said they would do it."
- **Percentages:** use numerals with "percent" mid-sentence ("95 percent of people"). Use the % symbol in headlines and tables ("95%").
- **Weights, times, dates:** state numerically (200 pounds, 5 hours).
- **Pluralize numerals** without an apostrophe: hundreds, thousands, 1920s.
- **Decades:** numerals (1920s).
- **Numbers over 1,000:** use commas (5,400).
- **Centuries:** spell out.
- **Round numbers:** fine to abbreviate with a capital letter, no space: 5.4M people.
- **Ranges** always use numerals, even for numbers under 10: "Between 1-10, rank this program."

## Dates and times

### Specific dates

- Month spelled out, day in figures: "February 12," "September 15."
- **No superscript** (no 22nd, no 3rd).

### Years

- **Year ranges:** four digits on both sides (2017–2020), en dash between.
- **Plural numerals:** no apostrophe (1920s).
- **Decades:** numerals (1920s).
- **Centuries:** spell out.

### Times

- Default timezone: **Pacific Time (PT)**. Use local timezone for regional audiences (ET for NYC, etc.).
- Use "a.m." and "p.m." — lowercase with periods. **Not** "AM/PM" or "am/pm."
- Keep it short: "5 a.m.," not "5:00 a.m."
- "Noon" and "midnight." Never "12 noon" or "12 midnight."
- Ranges: "2–3 p.m." or "7 a.m.–10:30 p.m."

## Lists

- First letter of each bullet: uppercase.
- Introduce lists with a colon.
- End punctuation in bullets **only when** the list items are complete sentences.

## URLs

- Shortest URL possible.
- Lowercase.
- No "http://" or trailing slash, unless required for the URL to work.

## "It" vs. "They" for companies

- A company or organization takes "**it**," not "they."
- Individuals within the company can be referred to as "they."
